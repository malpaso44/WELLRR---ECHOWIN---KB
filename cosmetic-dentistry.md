### 13. PORCELAIN VENEERS

* Thin porcelain shells bonded to the front of teeth to address chips, deep stains, gaps, or minor misalignment.
* Process may involve removing a small amount of enamel, taking a digital scan or mold, creating the porcelain restoration, and bonding it in place.
* Best suited for correcting multiple cosmetic issues at once.
* Source lifespan guidance: approximately 10–15 years with proper care.
* Veneer treatment involving enamel removal is irreversible.

### 14. DENTAL BONDING

* Uses tooth-colored composite resin to rebuild broken edges or close small gaps.
* Typically requires little to no enamel removal.
* Resin is applied, shaped, and hardened with a specialized light.
* Best suited for fast, lower-cost correction of individual chipped or cracked teeth.
* Source lifespan guidance: approximately 3–10 years and more prone to staining or chipping than porcelain.

### 15. CLEAR ALIGNERS

* Transparent removable trays used sequentially to move teeth gradually.
* Treatment planning uses 3D technology to map tooth movement.
* Patients change to new trays during treatment as directed.
* Used for crowded teeth, gaps, or bite issues.
* Long-term results depend on continued retainer use after treatment.

### 16. PROFESSIONAL TEETH WHITENING

* Uses concentrated whitening agents to address stains.
* The process may include gum protection, whitening gel, and specialized light activation.
* Used for stains related to coffee, wine, smoking, and aging.
* Source lifespan guidance: approximately 6 months–2 years depending heavily on diet and smoking habits.

### 17. SERVICE COMPARISON

* Single chipped tooth → Dental bonding → Very low invasiveness.
* Severe internal stains → Porcelain veneers → Moderate invasiveness; minor tooth shaping may be required.
* Crowded teeth → Clear aligners → Low invasiveness; removable trays.
* Quickly brighten dull smile → In-office whitening → Very low invasiveness; surface treatment.
* Whitening and clear aligners preserve natural tooth structure.
* Veneers may require enamel removal and can be irreversible.

### 18. SMILE MAKEOVER PROCESS

**Step 1 — Design & Test Drive**

* Dentist may use 3D digital scanning, photography, and Digital Smile Design software to plan the smile.
* A physical mock-up may allow the patient to preview the proposed appearance before treatment.

**Step 2 — Health & Alignment**

* Underlying gum disease, decay, or major alignment issues are addressed first.
* Clear aligners may be used before veneers when alignment could reduce unnecessary removal of healthy tooth structure.

**Step 3 — Whitening**

* When whitening is part of treatment, natural teeth may be whitened before final veneers or bonding are shade-matched.
* Porcelain and composite restorations cannot be bleached after fabrication.

**Step 4 — Gum Contouring**

* Gum contouring may be used to address a gummy or uneven gum line.
* This may occur before final restorations.

**Step 5 — Final Restorations**

* Teeth may be prepared if required.
* Final digital impressions may be taken.
* Veneers, crowns, or bonding are placed.
* Bite is adjusted as necessary.

**Goal**

* Create a natural-looking smile that harmonizes with the patient’s facial features.
